Security

Quarter checks your payments. It never touches your money.

Quarter sits between your payment file and your bank. Here is what it does with what passes through, and what is not in place yet.

Four promises.

Never holds or moves money

Quarter checks payment files. It never holds, moves or sends money, and never sends anything to your bank. The released file comes back to you.

Only the last 4 digits are shown

Account numbers are encrypted when stored. In the app and the API you see only the last 4 digits. The file you release and the Positive Pay file carry them whole, because your bank needs them.

Wages are not held up

Pay to an account on your employee list warns instead of holding. Pay to any other account is held. So are several entries to one account, a sanctions match, and pay to one account that reaches your two-person threshold.

No passwords, ever

Vendors confirm their details by logging in to their own bank, on their bank's screen. Neither you nor Quarter sees their password.

Built into the code today.

Each of these is in the API now. None of them is a plan.

Account numbers are sealed

Every bank account number, and every payment file you upload, is encrypted with AES-256-GCM before it is stored. Each sealed value opens only in its own record, so one copied into another record cannot be read, and the key can be changed without losing data. Matching uses a keyed fingerprint that cannot be turned back into the number.

A passkey for anyone who can move money

Once your payments are live, approvers and admins sign in with the emailed link and then a passkey on their own device. Approving, rejecting or releasing a payment, recording a call-back, and changing settings or people ask for the passkey again after 5 minutes. A person's first passkey counts only once another admin approves it, so someone who reads a clerk's email still cannot approve a payment.

Customers are isolated in the database

PostgreSQL row-level security confines every API request to your own organization, on top of the checks in the code. The network sees fingerprints and counts, not your vendors.

The record cannot be rewritten unnoticed

The application has no permission to change or delete audit log entries, events or verification evidence. Each audit entry carries the hash of the one before it, and Quarter emails each day’s latest hash outside the database, so a later rewrite of an earlier entry, even with direct database access, no longer matches the hash already sent. One kind of entry is checked less: where a privacy request replaced a vendor contact’s email, the check confirms the entry’s place in the chain and its shape, not its content.

Security events are kept and alerted

Passkey resets, API keys used from a new network, member and setting changes, a replaced employee list, new webhook endpoints, NetSuite changes, exports, releases and downloads of files with full account numbers are kept in their own tamper-evident chain. The serious ones are emailed to your admins and security contacts.

Webhooks are signed

HMAC SHA-256 over a timestamp and the raw body. In production, deliveries go only to public https addresses and never follow redirects.

Test and live never mix

A key's prefix must match its project: a test key never reaches live data, and a live key never reaches test data. A test project gets no answers from the fraud network.

Keys and links are stored as hashes

API keys, vendor verification links and email codes are kept only as hashes. Links expire after 14 days, and codes after 15 minutes.

A record you can show anyone.

  • Every payment run, and every finding on every payment in it
  • Every decision, with who made it, when, and the reason they gave
  • Every piece of verification evidence: email codes, bank confirmations and call-backs
  • Every change to a vendor or to bank details, with the history kept
  • Every release and every cancelled run, with who did it
  • Your written procedure and each yearly review of it

Written down, and yours to review.

We share these during your security or vendor review.

  • A written information security program, with a named owner, a risk assessment and a yearly review
  • Policies for access, changes, vulnerabilities, incidents, business continuity, retention and the outside services we use
  • A list of every outside service that handles your data, and notice to you before a new one starts
  • Answers to the CAIQ and SIG Lite security questionnaires, and a mapping to the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0

If something goes wrong, we tell you.

Our incident plan sets these times from when we find an incident. Your contract will repeat them.

  • Banks and credit unions: as soon as possible, and within 4 hours, when an incident disrupts or may disrupt our service to you for 4 hours or more
  • Every customer: within 24 hours when an incident puts your payments or your data at risk
  • A written account of what data was reached, whose, and what we did, within 72 hours

What is not in place yet.

Quarter is pre-launch. We would rather you hear it here than find it later.

  • Single sign-on through Google or Microsoft. People sign in by an emailed link, and approvers and admins in a live organization also with a passkey
  • Bank login confirmation against real banks, which waits on Plaid's production approval
  • A way for a business to dispute a fraud report about its account, needed before the network is used by customers
  • Terms, a privacy notice, a data processing agreement and a subprocessor list in effect. They are published as drafts, which take effect once the company is formed
  • Third-party audits or certifications, such as SOC 2, and an outside penetration test
  • Code review that our code host enforces. Today review before each change is our rule, not a technical block

Report a security issue.

If you find a weakness in Quarter, tell us before anyone else. A founder reads every report.

Security contact

Use the request form, choose Other as your role, and start your note with Security. Please do not use real bank details or another customer's data to test.
