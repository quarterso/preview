Security

Quarter checks your payments. It never touches your money.

Quarter sits between your payment file and your bank. Here is what it does with what passes through, and what is not in place yet.

Four promises.

Never holds or moves money

Quarter checks payment files. It never holds, moves or sends money, and never sends anything to your bank. The released file comes back to you.

Only the last 4 digits are shown

Account numbers are encrypted when stored. Everywhere you look, in the app and in the API, you see only the last 4 digits.

Counts, never who

When the network says an account was confirmed or reported by other businesses, it gives a count. It never says which business.

No passwords, ever

Vendors confirm their details by logging in to their own bank, on their bank's screen. Neither you nor Quarter sees their password.

Built into the code today.

Each of these is in the API now. None of them is a plan.

Account numbers are sealed

Every bank account number, and every payment file you upload, is encrypted with AES-256-GCM before it is stored. Matching uses a keyed fingerprint that cannot be turned back into the number.

Customers are isolated in the database

PostgreSQL row-level security confines every API request to your own organization, on top of the checks in the code. The network sees fingerprints and counts, not your vendors.

The record cannot be rewritten

The application has no permission to change or delete audit log entries, events or verification evidence. Not even a bug can rewrite what happened.

Webhooks are signed

HMAC SHA-256 over a timestamp and the raw body. Deliveries go only to https URLs and never follow redirects.

Test and live never mix

A key's prefix must match its project: a test key never reaches live data, and a live key never reaches test data.

Keys and links are stored as hashes

API keys, vendor verification links and email codes are kept only as hashes. Links expire after 14 days, and codes after 15 minutes.

A record you can show anyone.

  • Every payment run, and every finding on every payment in it
  • Every decision, with who made it, when, and the reason they gave
  • Every piece of verification evidence: email codes, bank confirmations and call-backs
  • Every change to a vendor or to bank details, with the history kept
  • Every release and every cancelled run, with who did it
  • Your written procedure and each yearly review of it

What is not in place yet.

Quarter is pre-launch. We would rather you hear it here than find it later.

  • Sign-in, roles and single sign-on for the app. Quarter issues API keys during early access
  • Bank login confirmation against real banks, which waits on production approval
  • A data processing agreement and a published sub-processor list
  • Third-party audits or certifications, such as SOC 2
