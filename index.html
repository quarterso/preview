Payments that just work. Every purchase becomes the right access, exactly once.

Your app sells a plan or credits through Stripe, the App Store or Google Play. Quarter checks each payment with the store, unlocks what was bought, and takes it away on cancel, refund or a failed renewal.

Your app asks one question: what does this user have right now?

The problem

Taking the payment is the easy part. Getting access right is not.

Payment code written in an afternoon, often by an AI tool, handles the happy path. Everything after it lands in your support inbox.

What goes wrong

Paid, and still locked out.

Credited twice for one purchase.

Cancelled, and still on the paid plan.

Store events arrive late, twice, or out of order

Stores retry and reorder what they send. Code that trusts the order it receives grants the wrong thing.

Every store works differently

Stripe sends webhooks, Apple sends signed notifications, Google sends a token you have to look up.

Refunds and failed renewals get missed

Access that is never taken away is a plan you give away for free.

AI credits need a real ledger

Two requests at once can spend the same credit twice, or push a balance below zero.

How it works

From a payment to the right access.

The stores tell Quarter what happened. Quarter checks it with the store and keeps access in step.

  1. A user pays

    Your app sells a plan or credits through Stripe Checkout, the App Store or Google Play.

  2. Quarter checks the store

    It verifies what the store sent, then asks the store for the latest state. Nothing unlocks unless the store confirms it.

  3. Access changes once

    The plan unlocks or the credits are added. The same payment seen twice changes nothing.

  4. Your app asks

    One call answers what this user has right now. Or read it from a table in your own database.

  5. Access follows the plan

    Renewals extend it. A cancel, a refund or a failed renewal takes it away at the right time.

For you, and for the AI tool that writes your code

One question, one answer.

Your app never parses a store receipt or orders webhooks. It asks Quarter what this user has right now, and gets the answer the stores agree on.

  • Ask what a user has with one GET request.
  • Hold credits while an AI call runs, and spend only what it used.
  • Or skip the API and read a table in your own Postgres or Supabase.

Credits for AI apps

Charge for what each AI call used. Never more.

Hold the most a call can cost before it runs. When it ends, spend what it used and the rest goes back.

  • A failed call costs nothing: everything held goes back.
  • Parallel calls cannot overspend, because held credits leave the balance at once.
  • A retry with the same idempotency key never charges twice.
  • A hold that is never settled is released when it expires.

Who it is for

You built a paid app with an AI tool. Now people are paying.

Quarter starts with people shipping paid apps built with Lovable, Replit, Base44, Bolt and v0. Those apps can take a payment in an afternoon. Quarter keeps every payment after that turning into the right access.

Trust

Quarter keeps access. Your money stays yours.

Never holds your money

Payments stay in your own Stripe, Apple or Google account. Quarter never holds or moves money.

Never sees card data

Quarter reads store ids and events. Card numbers never reach it.

A read-only Stripe key

Quarter uses only a restricted key that can read. It cannot charge, refund or pay out.

Your user ids, nothing more

Quarter stores your own user ids, not names or emails.

Erasure and export

One call erases a user, and one call exports everything Quarter holds about them.

Live and planned

What works today, and what does not yet.

Quarter is pre-launch. Everything in the first list is built and tested against simulated Stripe, Apple and Google stores. No paying app runs on it yet. Everything in the second list is planned and not built.

Built and tested

  • Stripe. Checkout, subscriptions and one-time payments, read with your read-only restricted key.
  • App Store. Apple's signed transactions and server notifications, checked against Apple's certificates.
  • Google Play. Subscriptions and one-time products such as credit packs, each confirmed with a fetch from the Play API.
  • One access call. Entitlements, credit balances and purchases for one user.
  • Credit holds for AI calls. Hold credits while a call runs and spend what it used. A failed call costs nothing.
  • Credits. Atomic spends with an idempotency key. A balance never goes negative and a spend never happens twice.
  • Locked-out check. One call asks every store again about everything a user bought.
  • Mirror to your database. One row per user in a Postgres or Supabase table that Quarter creates.
  • Signed webhooks. Access, subscription and credit events, with retries and replay.
  • Erasure and export. One call each, for any user who asks.
  • TypeScript SDK. Access, credit holds, purchases and webhooks, with last known access during an outage. Coming to npm.

Planned, not available yet

  • The console: apps, products, customer lookup with a timeline, and webhooks
  • Self-serve sign-up and API keys
