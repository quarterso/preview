How it works

Every payment, turned into the right access, exactly once.

The stores tell Quarter what happened. Quarter checks it with the store, changes access once, and keeps it in step for the whole life of the plan.

The path

Five steps, all of them built.

  1. A user pays

    Your app sells a plan or credits through Stripe Checkout, the App Store or Google Play.

  2. Quarter checks the store

    It verifies what the store sent, then asks the store for the latest state. Nothing unlocks unless the store confirms it.

  3. Access changes once

    The plan unlocks or the credits are added. The same payment seen twice changes nothing.

  4. Your app asks

    One call answers what this user has right now. Or read it from a table in your own database.

  5. Access follows the plan

    Renewals extend it. A cancel, a refund or a failed renewal takes it away at the right time.

Worked example

One Pro subscription, step by step.

An AI app sells Pro with 200 credits a month on the App Store. The details are illustrative.

  1. Setup
    product

    You connect your stores and say what each product grants

    Give Quarter a read-only Stripe restricted key, an App Store key or a Google Play service account. Then say what each store product grants: the pro entitlement, 200 credits a month, or both.

    POST /v1/apps/{app}/products
  2. Purchase
    subscription.started

    A user subscribes to Pro on their iPhone

    Your app reports the signed transaction as soon as the App Store hands it over. Quarter checks Apple's signature, records the purchase once, unlocks pro and adds 200 credits.

    POST /v1/apps/{app}/purchases
  3. Check
    access.changed

    Your app asks what the user has

    Before it shows a Pro feature, your app asks Quarter. Or it reads the row Quarter keeps for this user in your own database.

    GET /v1/apps/{app}/customers/{user}/access
  4. Spend
    credits.spent

    The user runs an AI generation

    Your backend spends one credit, with the generation's id as the idempotency key. A retried request spends nothing more. If the balance is too low, the spend is refused and nothing changes.

    POST /v1/apps/{app}/customers/{user}/credits/spend
  5. Renewal
    subscription.renewed

    A month later, the plan renews

    Apple tells Quarter, and Quarter checks it. Access runs to the new period end, and this month's credits are added.

When the plan does not simply renew

subscription.billing_issue

The renewal payment fails. The user keeps access while the store's grace period lasts. If the payment never succeeds, access ends.

subscription.cancelled

The user cancels. They keep access until the end of the period they paid for, and then it expires.

subscription.refunded

The payment is refunded. Access ends now, and the unspent credits it bought are taken back.

The answer

Everything your app needs to decide, in one response.

No receipts to parse and no webhooks to put in order. This is what your app reads for one user.

  • Entitlements

    Each one with its status, when it expires, and whether it will renew.

  • Credit balances

    One balance per currency you define, such as credits or tokens.

  • Purchases

    Every subscription and one-time purchase, from any of the three stores.

  • Events

    Every change, sent to your webhooks as it happens.

  • Your own table

    The same answer, as one row per user in your Postgres or Supabase.

The rules

Correct when the stores are not tidy.

  • Nothing unlocks unless the store confirmed it: Apple signed it, or Quarter fetched it from Google or Stripe.
  • Every store record is kept once and never changed. Names, emails and addresses are dropped before it is stored.
  • An older record never moves access backwards, whatever order the stores send them in.
  • The same payment applied twice changes nothing and sends nothing.
  • A credit balance never goes below zero, and a spend with the same idempotency key happens once.
  • Credits bought on the App Store never expire, as Apple requires.

When a user does something unusual

A user says they paid and are locked out
One call asks every store again about everything they bought. A store that does not answer never takes access away.
A user buys before signing in
One call links the anonymous id to their account, with every purchase and credit.
You want to give someone Pro for free
Grant the entitlement for a set number of days, or until you take it back.
Quarter cannot be reached
The SDK answers with the last known access for up to an hour, so an outage never locks out a paying user. Writes never fall back.
A user restores purchases
Your app reports the purchase again. Quarter recognises it and grants nothing twice.
A user asks to be forgotten
One call erases them, and removes their row from your mirror table.

Live and planned

What works today, and what does not yet.

Every purchase Quarter has handled so far came from simulated Stripe, Apple and Google stores in its tests. The first real app comes with early access.

Built and tested

  • Stripe. Checkout, subscriptions and one-time payments, read with your read-only restricted key.
  • App Store. Apple's signed transactions and server notifications, checked against Apple's certificates.
  • Google Play. Subscriptions and one-time products such as credit packs, each confirmed with a fetch from the Play API.
  • One access call. Entitlements, credit balances and purchases for one user.
  • Credit holds for AI calls. Hold credits while a call runs and spend what it used. A failed call costs nothing.
  • Credits. Atomic spends with an idempotency key. A balance never goes negative and a spend never happens twice.
  • Locked-out check. One call asks every store again about everything a user bought.
  • Mirror to your database. One row per user in a Postgres or Supabase table that Quarter creates.
  • Signed webhooks. Access, subscription and credit events, with retries and replay.
  • Erasure and export. One call each, for any user who asks.
  • TypeScript SDK. Access, credit holds, purchases and webhooks, with last known access during an outage. Coming to npm.

Planned, not available yet

  • The console: apps, products, customer lookup with a timeline, and webhooks
  • Self-serve sign-up and API keys
