How it works

From the file you were about to send, to a file you can trust.

Quarter sits between a payment and the place that sends it: your bank, card issuer or wallet. Nothing about how you pay changes, except that a person sees the payments that look wrong before they go. Here is a bank payment run, start to finish.

The path

Five steps, all of them built.

  1. Quarter sees the payment

    With NetSuite connected, Quarter reads your open vendor bills every 5 minutes. Without it, upload the NACHA file, CSV or check register you were about to send.

  2. Every payment is checked

    Is the payee a real vendor, were these bank details confirmed, does the amount look like the ones before it.

  3. A person decides

    Held payments wait with the reason and the evidence. Approve or reject, with a note. Large ones need a second person.

  4. Pay as you do today

    In NetSuite, a held bill stays on Payment Hold until someone decides. With a file, Quarter gives it back without the rejected payments, totals recomputed, and you send it to your bank.

  5. Everything is recorded

    Who checked, who approved, which evidence. Ready for your bank, your auditor or your insurer.

Worked example

One bank change request, one Friday run.

The companies, people and amounts are illustrative. The steps and the messages are the real ones.

  1. Wed 10:02
    bank details changed

    A bank change request arrives

    An email asks Northfield Manufacturing to pay Bayline Industrial Supply at a new account. The AP clerk records the new details in Quarter. From this moment, every payment to that account is held until someone confirms it with Bayline.

  2. Wed 10:04
    link sent

    Quarter sends Bayline a link

    To the contact Northfield already had for Bayline, not to the address that asked for the change. Bayline can confirm by logging in to its own bank. Nobody at Bayline does, because Bayline never asked for a change.

  3. Fri 3:40
    1 held

    The Friday run is uploaded

    Fourteen payments, $286,412.50, as a NACHA file from the ERP. Thirteen are clear. One is held, with the reason in plain words.

    bank details changed 2 days ago and were not confirmed with the vendor
  4. Fri 3:48
    rejected

    The controller decides

    Dana, the controller, reads the evidence and calls Bayline on the number in the vendor list from before the change. Bayline never changed banks. She rejects the payment, writes down why, and records it as a fraud attempt. The console counts it among the month’s catches.

  5. Fri 3:50
    released

    The run is released, clean

    Quarter gives back the file without the rejected payment: 13 payments, $238,162.50, with the NACHA totals recomputed. Dana uploads it to the bank as she always does.

When it goes another way

verified

The vendor really did change banks and confirms through their bank login before the run. Their payment is no longer held, and it carries a note that the details changed recently.

approved

The controller has other proof and approves, with a reason. At or above your two-person threshold, a second, different person must approve as well.

cancelled

The whole run is cancelled before release. Nothing is sent, and the record of what was checked stays.

How a vendor proves it

A scammer can write the email and answer the phone. They cannot log in to your supplier's bank.

So that is where the proof comes from. Quarter sends your vendor a link. They log in to their own bank, and if the account is the one on file, in their name, it is confirmed. Payments to it stop being held.

  1. You send the vendor a linkFrom Quarter, to the contact you already had for them. Not to the address that asked for the change.
  2. A code to their company emailQuarter records whether the inbox is on the vendor's own email domain. On its own, that proves little, so it never verifies anything by itself.
  3. They log in to their own bankThrough Plaid, on their bank's own login screen. If the account is the one on file and the owner's name matches the vendor, it is confirmed.

Prefer to call? Record a call-back to a number you held before the change. Quarter keeps who called, which number and where it came from, so the evidence outlives the person who made the call.

How call-backs are recorded

Call-backs

A call-back counts only if you chose the number.

The number in a change request belongs to whoever sent it. Calling it proves nothing. Call the vendor on a number you had before the change, and Quarter records it as evidence.

What Quarter records for each call

  • Who called, and who answered
  • Which number, and where it came from: your vendor list before the change, a signed contract, the vendor's public website or an earlier invoice
  • What they said, as pass or fail
  • Never a recording. Just the facts, kept for good

The checks

33 checks, each with a reason in plain words.

A hold stops the payment until a person decides. A warn shows the reason and lets it through. You set each one.

  • unknown_payeeholdThe payee is not a vendor you have on file.
  • vendor_blockedholdYou blocked this vendor from being paid. Always on
  • unverified_accountholdThese bank details were never confirmed with the vendor.
  • account_changed_recentlyholdThe bank details changed recently and nobody confirmed them with the vendor.
  • account_not_on_fileholdThe payment goes to an account that is not the one on file for this vendor.
  • name_mismatchwarnThe name on the payment does not match the vendor it pays.
  • first_paymentwarnThis is the first payment to this vendor.
  • amount_unusualwarnFar more than this vendor is usually paid.
  • duplicate_paymentholdThe same payment appears twice, or was already paid in the last 7 days.
  • shared_accountholdAnother of your vendors is paid at this same account.
  • network_flaggedwarnA business on Quarter reported this account in connection with a payment fraud.
  • sanctions_matchholdThe payee name resembles a name on OFAC's SDN or Consolidated lists. Always on
  • routing_invalidholdThe routing number is not a valid US bank routing number.
  • iban_invalidholdThe IBAN on an international payment fails its checksum.
  • check_number_reusedholdThis check number was already used on the same account.
  • payee_name_alteredwarnThe payee line on a check is close to the vendor's name on file, but not the same.
  • just_under_thresholdwarnA check for just under the amount that needs two approvers.
  • employee_account_matchholdThe account paid, or the vendor's account on file, is on your list of employee bank accounts.
  • payroll_account_unlistedholdPayroll pays an account not on your employee list, pays one account more than once, or has no list to compare with. Always on
  • vendor_dormant_reactivatedwarnA vendor not paid for a year has new bank details since its last payment.
  • split_below_thresholdwarnPayments to one vendor within 7 days, each under the two-approver amount, together reach it.
  • vendor_new_paid_fastwarnA vendor added in the last 14 days is first paid by the person who added it.
  • request_domain_lookalikewarnThe request for new bank details came by email from a domain that imitates the vendor's, or from a domain other than the vendor's.
  • request_domain_newwarnThe request for new bank details came from a domain registered in the 90 days before the request.
  • request_domain_no_dmarcwarnThe request came from the vendor's domain, which does not refuse mail forged in its name.
  • request_email_suspiciousholdThe forwarded email asking for these bank details failed Quarter's checks.
  • second_person_requiredholdAt or above the two-person threshold, one of several payments to one payee in 7 days that add up to it, or paid by the person who changed the vendor's bank details. Always on
  • agent_over_limitholdAn AI agent asked to pay more than its limit for one payment, or more than its daily limit. Always on
  • agent_payee_not_allowedholdAn AI agent asked to pay a vendor or merchant category that is not on its allowed list. Always on
  • payout_details_changedholdA platform payout to a payee whose bank details changed in the last 72 hours.
  • payout_new_payee_largeholdThe first payout to a payee is $2,500 or more.
  • payout_account_fan_inholdThree or more connected accounts pay out to the same bank account.
  • payout_velocity_spikeholdA connected account's payouts in the last 24 hours reach $10,000 and are more than 5 times its usual day.
Cooling period
A changed account is held until someone confirms it. After that, payments to it carry a warning for this long: 10 days unless you choose otherwise.
Unusual amount
A payment more than 3 times the usual for that vendor, once there are at least three payments to compare.
Two-person threshold
At or above this amount, every payment is held, and approving it takes two different people. So do smaller payments to one payee that add up to it within 7 days. $50,000 unless you choose otherwise.
Hold, warn or off
For each check, except three that always hold: a blocked vendor, the sanctions list, and a second person for large payments.

The record

The documented process, written for you.

Nacha's fraud-monitoring rule asks every business that sends ACH for a risk-based process, reviewed every year. Quarter writes your procedure from the settings you chose, records each yearly review, and keeps every check, decision and piece of evidence in an audit log that nothing deletes.

The procedure and the audit log

Promises the code keeps

  • A held payment is never in a released file unless a person approved it.
  • A released file's totals always match the payments in it.
  • A run cannot change after it is released.
  • Verification evidence is only ever added to, never edited or removed.
  • Each customer's data is isolated in the database, not only in the code.
  • The network never tells you which business confirmed or reported an account.

Built and planned

What works today, and what does not yet.

Quarter is not open yet. Everything in the first list is built and tested. No customer runs on it yet. Everything in the second list is planned and not built.

Built and tested

  • Payment run checks. NACHA files, CSV exports and JSON, matched to vendors and checked before release. 33 checks in all.
  • Check runs and Positive Pay. Your check register screened before printing, then your bank's Positive Pay file in a generic CSV, Jack Henry Banno, Citizens Bank or F&M Bank layout, or your own bank's layout. You upload the file; Quarter never sends it.
  • Insider controls. Nobody can approve a payment to a vendor whose bank details they changed in the last 90 days, or release it unless someone else approved it. Nobody confirms bank details they entered. Payments to employee accounts are held. A report shows every vendor change by person.
  • NetSuite connection. Vendors and their bank details, read every hour. Built and tested against a sample account, and in testing before the first pilot. It has not yet run against a real NetSuite account.
  • Holds written back into NetSuite. Quarter checks every open vendor bill and sets the bill's own Payment Hold, so NetSuite does not pay a held bill until a person decides in Quarter. Someone who clears the hold in NetSuite is reported, and the hold goes back on. No file upload. Built and tested against a simulated NetSuite account; not yet run against a real one.
  • Payment checks. One wire, RTP, FedNow or international payment checked before it is keyed into the bank portal. When it is clear or approved, Quarter gives a release code for the memo.
  • Card and stablecoin payments. A card payment checked by its merchant category code, and a stablecoin payment by its chain and wallet address, through the same API and the same record. There is no console form for them yet.
  • AI agents as payers. Each agent has its own key, a per-payment and daily limit, and allowed payees or merchant categories. A payment over a limit or off the list is always held, and the agent's key cannot decide it.
  • Sign-in, roles and API keys. Sign-in by email link, with a passkey as well for approvers and admins once payments are live, and again before each money decision. Admin, approver and viewer roles, and API keys created by an admin. Quarter staff switch a project to live.
  • Holds and decisions. People signed in to Quarter approve or reject with a reason, with a second, different approver at or above your threshold. An API key never decides.
  • Release. The file back without rejected payments, with NACHA batch and file totals recomputed.
  • Vendors and bank details. Import your vendor list, with every change to bank details kept as history.
  • Vendor verification links. Email code, then bank login confirmation. Built and tested against a simulated bank connection.
  • Call-back records. Who called, which number and where it came from, with the result.
  • The written procedure. Generated from your settings, with the yearly review recorded.
  • Nacha fraud monitoring program. One export that maps your controls to Nacha's fraud monitoring rule: the procedure, the checks in force, a year of counts and the yearly review.
  • NetSuite SuiteApp. Holds a vendor's bills inside NetSuite the moment its bank details change, until Quarter has verified them. Tested outside NetSuite.
  • Settlement matching. Bank and card statements read back, in CSV or BAI2, with money that left without Quarter flagged.
  • Wallet screening. A stablecoin payment to a wallet OFAC lists is held, whatever case the address is written in.
  • Lithic card authorizations. Lithic asks Quarter while each card purchase is authorized, and a held one is declined.
  • Audit log. Every action, every person, every piece of evidence, each entry chained by its hash to the one before.
  • Account checks and fraud reports. Counts from other businesses, never names. Only live projects contribute, so the network starts empty.
  • Signed webhooks. Held payments, released runs, verified vendors and changed bank details.

Planned, not available yet

  • Card authorizations from issuers other than Lithic. Today another issuer calls the payment check from its own endpoint
  • Bank login confirmation with real banks, once Plaid approves production access
  • Connections to other ERPs and accounting tools. Today you upload the file, or connect NetSuite
  • Single sign-on
