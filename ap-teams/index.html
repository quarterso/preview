A payment week with Quarter

Your AP clerk looks at the few payments that need it.

Many bank changes are real. The ones to stop are changed and not confirmed with the vendor. Quarter holds those, and only warns on the rest.

  1. Bills come in as they do today

    Upload the NACHA file, CSV or check register before it goes to your bank, from any accounting system. On NetSuite, Quarter can instead read your open vendor bills every 5 minutes and check each one.

  2. The risky ones are held, with the reason

    A held bill stays on NetSuite’s own Payment Hold. The queue puts the payment with the most points first, with reasons in plain words: “bank details changed 2 days ago and were not confirmed with the vendor”.

  3. The vendor confirms, or you call back

    Send the vendor a link to log in to its own bank, or record a call-back to a number you had before the change. A number from the change request is refused.

  4. A named person decides

    Approve or reject with a reason and a passkey. Above your threshold, or for smaller payments to one payee that add up to it, two different people. Nobody approves a vendor whose bank details they changed.

  5. Month end, the summary

    What was held, what each hold turned out to be, and any payment that left and later proved wrong. The record is already there when someone asks.

For the controller

The controls you would design yourself, run on every payment.

Segregation of duties, enforced

Whoever entered new bank details cannot confirm them, and for 90 days cannot approve a payment to that vendor or release it alone. For changes made in NetSuite, Quarter reads who made them from the system notes.

Where the change request came from

Each bank change records how it arrived. For email, Quarter compares the sender’s domain with the vendor’s and flags a lookalike, a newly registered domain or one with no DMARC policy.

Check runs and Positive Pay

Your check register screened before the checks are printed, then the Positive Pay file in your bank’s layout. You upload it to the bank as today.

Wires and instant payments

One payment at a time, checked before someone keys it into the bank portal, with a release code for the memo.

The proof

What you hand your bank, auditor or insurer.

Call-back records

Who called, which number, where that number came from, and what the vendor said. A number taken from the change request is refused.

A claim file per vendor

Every bank change, how the request arrived, the call-back or bank login that confirmed it, and every payment released after it. One download.

An audit log you can check

Every action and every person, each entry chained by its hash to the one before, with the latest hash sent outside the database each day.

Outcomes and misses

What each hold turned out to be, and any payment that left and later proved wrong, in a monthly summary.

A report mapped to Nacha's 2026 rules

Your change controls, account validation, call-backs, dual control and yearly review, with a year of counts. It supports your process. It does not make anyone compliant.

Built first for companies with

  • NetSuite, with vendor bank details kept in NetSuite
  • Vendors paid by ACH, check or wire from your own bank
  • An AP team of 1 to 4 people and a controller who signs off
  • No payables network: you do not pay through BILL, Ramp, AvidXchange or Paymode

What to know first

  • The NetSuite payment flow is tested against a simulated NetSuite account. It has not yet run against a real one.
  • Vendor bank login runs on Plaid’s test environment until Plaid approves production.
  • Quarter offers no guarantee. You decide every payment.
  • Payroll to an account on your employee list warns and is not held. Pay to any other account is held.
  • It helps you show a fraud-monitoring process. It does not make you compliant with Nacha’s rules.
