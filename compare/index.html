How we compare

Where Quarter fits next to what you already have.

Each description below is what the product says about itself on its own pages, read on October 3, 2026. We say when another option is the better fit. Prices and features change, so check the source before you decide.

Options compared

OptionWhat it does, by its own descriptionThe better fit whenWhere Quarter differs
Your bank's Positive PayThe bank compares each check presented with the issue file you sent it, and asks you about the ones that do not match. Source 1.Always keep it. It catches altered and counterfeit checks at the bank, which Quarter cannot see.Works before the bank: it screens the check register before the checks are printed and writes the issue file in your bank’s layout. It also checks ACH payments and wires to vendors, which check Positive Pay does not cover.
Payee name checks at your bankThe Federal Reserve's Payee Name Verification lets a bank compare a payee name with the account's past FedACH payments. Source 2.Use it where your bank offers it. It is evidence about the account.Adds the controls around the payment: holds on changed and unconfirmed details, two people, segregation of duties and the record. A brand-new account has no payment history to match.
Ramp Bill PayReviews each AP transaction it processes against 60 signals and flags changed or unverified vendor bank details. Bill pay is on Ramp’s free plan, with per-payment fees. Source 3, 4.If you pay, or are ready to pay, your bills through Ramp.Checks the payments you already send from your own bank and NetSuite, without moving them to a payables platform.
BILLSends a $0.01 test deposit to confirm a vendor’s bank account can receive payments, for payments made through BILL. Source 5.If you pay vendors through BILL.Never moves money, so it confirms details by the vendor’s own bank login or a recorded call-back instead of a deposit, for payments made outside BILL.
EftsureVerifies payee details against a large network, with segregation of duties and a guarantee of up to $1M on verified payments. Inside NetSuite it flags high-risk transactions and can block them by rule, and it can send validated files from NetSuite to the bank. It says most businesses are running within a few weeks. Source 6, 7, 8.If you want a guarantee, a large verification network, international vendor coverage, or files sent to your bank for you.Offers no guarantee, and its network starts empty. It never sends anything to your bank. Built for a mid-market AP team that pays from its own bank: you connect NetSuite yourself, and Quarter holds risky bills with NetSuite's own Payment Hold box. It starts with a trial or a 60-day pilot.
TrustpairValidates supplier bank accounts in 190 countries for large companies, with 20+ connectors and up to $1M of liability protection. Basware agreed to buy it in 2026. Source 9, 10.If you are a large company with suppliers in many countries.Focused on US companies paying by ACH, check and wire from their own bank, with check runs and Positive Pay files included.
A written call-back policyNacha's guidance for originators names out-of-band verification and dual control as controls, scaled to the size of the business. Source 11.It is the control. Keep it, whatever tool you use.Holds the payment until the call is done, refuses a number taken from the change request, and records who called, which number and where it came from.

Fair to say

What Quarter does not have yet.

If one of these is a must for you, another option above may suit you better today. See what is built and what is planned.

  • A guarantee or reimbursement of any loss
  • A verification network with scale. Quarter’s starts empty
  • A SOC 2 report or an outside penetration test
  • Customers. Quarter is pre-launch
  • Connectors beyond NetSuite, and bank login with real banks until Plaid approves production
